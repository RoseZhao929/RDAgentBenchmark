\section{Main Results}
\label{sec-6}

We report cross-ontology Recall@1 (R@1) across agents, backbones, and datasets, followed by backbone sensitivity and cost--accuracy analysis.

\subsection{Headline R@1 Matrix}
\label{sec-6-1}
The average is reported only for LLM configurations covering all four development datasets and is not comparable to the two-dataset coverage of LIRICAL and VC-RDAgent. On RareBench, DeepRare leads the LLM systems at every backbone, spanning .158--.297, through the HPO-plus-variant channel that the general scaffolds lack. All V4-Pro cells use the reasoning-disabled setting. All four MAI-DxO backbones complete every layer, but the system is the weakest scaffold throughout, at or near zero on the note layer and on most HPO-input cells. The PMC-OA column is read as a within-layer pre- versus post-cutoff comparison rather than as a cross-agent ranking, and its higher absolute level reflects the diagnostic legibility of published case reports rather than easier diseases.

\subsection{Backbone--Scaffold Interaction}
\label{sec-6-2}

Table~\ref{tbl:bbsens} summarizes PP-Store sensitivity to backbone choice using the cells in Table~\ref{tbl:main}. Gemini is strongest on PP-Store for the LLM control and four of the five scaffolded agents; MAI-DxO instead peaks with V4-Flash at .16. Backbone ranges are .02 for MAI-DxO, .04--.05 for the LLM control, MDAgents, and MedAgents, .08 for AgentClinic, and .12 for DeepRare. The ordering changes elsewhere. On the MIMIC-IV-Note probe the four backbones are statistically indistinguishable for the unscaffolded control, spanning only .353--.373; GPT-5 minimal is by far the strongest for MDAgents at .435 against .317--.368 for the others; and V4-Pro is strongest for AgentClinic at .216, against .048 for GPT-5 minimal. Backbone effects are therefore scaffold- and dataset-dependent, and because several bootstrap intervals overlap we do not treat small gaps as definitive rankings.

\subsection{Cost--Accuracy Trade-offs}
\label{sec-6-3}

The evaluation comprises 97,985 predictions at a total API cost of \$297.89, or \$0.0030 per prediction including offline runs. Per-prediction cost spans more than an order of magnitude: DeepSeek V4-Flash is cheapest (\$0.00032), then V4-Pro reasoning-off (\$0.00098), Gemini Flash (\$0.00341), and GPT-5 minimal the most expensive (\$0.00825); the classical/offline baselines incur no API cost. Relative to V4-Flash, V4-Pro costs $3.1\times$ more, Gemini $10.7\times$ more, and GPT-5 minimal $25.8\times$ more; GPT-5 is also $8.4\times$ more expensive than V4-Pro. As Figure~\ref{fig:main6}(b) shows, this cost ordering does not induce a matching accuracy ordering. The evaluation used 83\% of the preregistered \$360 budget.

The same mismatch holds for wall-clock cost: median per-case latency spans two orders of magnitude, from $3$\,s for the single-LLM control to $244$\,s for MAI-DxO, roughly $80\times$ slower, yet the heavy scaffolds match or trail the control on R@1.

\subsection{Headline Findings}
\label{sec-6-4}

\paragraph{F1: Classical and offline methods outperform scaffolded LLMs on HPO input.}
On PP-Store, LIRICAL reaches .47 R@1 and VC-RDAgent .44, compared with .30 for the best scaffolded LLM cell, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:main6}(a). The resulting 17-point gap between LIRICAL and MedAgents holds across the full evaluation. RareBench shows a related pattern: DeepRare leads at .16--.30, with VC-RDAgent at .28 and LIRICAL at .23, while all other LLM scaffolds remain at or below .10. The magnitude of the RareBench gap is partly attributable to ontology alignment, as examined under F5.

\paragraph{F2: Multi-agent scaffolding does not reliably beat a single-LLM control.}
The same pattern holds on two independent datasets, PP-Store with Gemini Flash and the MIMIC-IV-Note probe averaged over four backbones, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:main6}(d). No scaffold clears the no-scaffold control by a meaningful margin: MedAgents and MDAgents stay within $1.4$ points of the control on both datasets, while the heavier-orchestration scaffolds fall well below it. AgentClinic drops $7.8$ points on PP-Store and $22.4$ on MIMIC, and MAI-DxO drops $14.8$ and $36.1$. On the MIMIC-IV-Note matrix the unscaffolded control at mean R@1 .364 is statistically tied with the best light scaffolds MedAgents .350 and MDAgents .367 under overlapping CIs, and far above the heavy ones AgentClinic .140 and DeepRare .011. The evidence thus supports a conditional, often negative scaffold effect in which heavy orchestration consistently hurts, rather than a general multi-agent advantage.

\paragraph{F3: V4-Flash is cost efficient but not quality equivalent to Gemini.}
V4-Flash costs \$0.00032 per prediction, compared with \$0.00341 for Gemini. On PP-Store, it trails Gemini by 3 points for the LLM control and MDAgents, 5 for MedAgents, and 8 for AgentClinic. On the MIMIC-IV-Note probe the sign flips for the light-coordination scaffolds---V4-Flash is 2 points \emph{below} Gemini for the LLM control and 6 below for MedAgents, but 5 points \emph{above} it for MDAgents and 4 above for AgentClinic. The appropriate conclusion is therefore lower cost with an agent- and modality-dependent accuracy trade-off, not quality parity.

\paragraph{F4: GPT-5 minimal reasoning carries the highest cost without a consistent accuracy advantage.}
GPT-5 minimal is competitive in selected cells and holds the single best MIMIC-IV-Note cell in the whole matrix, MDAgents at .435, but it is not the best backbone for any scaffold on PP-Store: Gemini leads four of the five scaffolded agents, while V4-Flash leads MAI-DxO, and it is the \emph{worst} of the four backbones for AgentClinic at .13 on PP-Store and .048 on the note probe. At $25.8\times$ the per-prediction cost of V4-Flash, these isolated gains do not establish a favorable overall trade-off. Isolating the reasoning channel confirms this: enabling reasoning for the V4-Pro single-LLM control changes R@1 by only +.008 while increasing latency by $10$--$40\times$. These findings apply to the evaluated adapters and token limits rather than to unconstrained GPT-5 quality in general.

\paragraph{F5: RareBench exposes an evaluator--ontology interaction.}
RareBench is uniquely difficult for general LLM scaffolds but remains tractable for systems operating directly over HPO and disease ontologies. Error analysis identifies sibling-level ORPHA distinctions and incomplete OMIM--Orphanet mappings as recurrent failure modes. For example, \texttt{Methylmalonic acidemia with homocystinuria} (ORPHA:26) and \texttt{Vitamin B12-unresponsive methylmalonic acidemia} (ORPHA:27) are clinically related but receive different exact identifiers. Classical and HPO-centered pipelines can bypass part of this mismatch through OMIM identifiers and Orphanet name matching. Cross-ontology fuzzy matching recovers 1--8 R@1 points across affected cells but does not close the performance gap. We therefore report both exact and cross-ontology scores and treat the result as an interaction among model behavior, cross-ontology mapping, and evaluator policy rather than as a pure model failure.

\paragraph{Prevalence and pre-registered tests.}
Stratifying by Orphanet prevalence tier sharpens F1, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:main6}(e). LLM agents peak on common and ultra-rare tiers but fall to R@1 .22 on super-rare disease, whereas the classical baselines \emph{rise} to .50 there---a 28-point reversal on exactly the rarest cases. Across the pre-registered family in Figure~\ref{fig:main6}(c), five of the six testable hypotheses survive Holm--Bonferroni correction, including the classical-over-LLM super-rare gap H1 and the genotype-channel lift H2; only the faithfulness--accuracy decoupling H10 fails and is reported as exploratory. Adding a structured variant block to the phenotype-only input raises the single-LLM control's R@1 on PP-Store from .30 to .49, a consistent genotype-channel lift of about 20 points ($z=6.4$), with DeepRare showing a comparable gain.

Swapping the P4 trace judge from the same model family to a cross-family model (Figure~\ref{fig:main6}(f)) moves several P4 dimensions by .40--.50 points, indicating an LLM-as-judge self-preference effect; we therefore report trace scores as exploratory and rely on deterministic matching for the headline metrics.

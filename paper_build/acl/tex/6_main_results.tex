Cost per prediction spans more than an order of magnitude across backbones:
DS V4-Flash is the cheapest hosted backbone (\$0.00040/pred), DS V4-Pro
reasoning-off is the frontier-tier cost-efficiency sweet spot (\$0.00088/pred),
and GPT-5 minimal is \textasciitilde20\ensuremath{\times} more expensive than V4-Flash with no consistent R@1 advantage (it ties medagents and is worst on agentclinic --- see F4). The

\subsection{Headline Findings}\label{sec-6-4}

\begin{quote}
V4-Pro re-run \textbf{reasoning-off} after root-causing an unbounded-reasoning
starvation bug (\Cref{sec-5-2} Methods note 2). (2) \textbf{PP-Store/RareArena harmonized to a \textgreater{} common N=2000 sample} across all backbones (earlier cells ranged 500--4589 due
to historical over-runs, breaking cross-backbone comparability). All Table-1
pp/rarearena cells now report on identical case-ids. Net effect: R@1 estimates
settled \textasciitilde2--4 pp below the small-sample values (the 500-case samples were mildly
optimistic), so F1 \emph{widens} (17--18 pp) and F4's ``GPT-5 best for medagents'' no
longer holds (medagents Gemini 0.30 \ensuremath{\geq} GPT-5 0.28). F2/F3 directions unchanged.
\end{quote}

\textbf{F1: Classical / offline beats scaffolded LLMs on HPO-input datasets.}
LIRICAL (Bayesian) R@1=0.47, VC-RDAgent Stage 1 (offline IC+Poincar\'e)
R@1=0.44 on Phenopacket-Store (common N=2000 sample), against the best LLM
cell (medagents \ensuremath{\times} Gemini R@1=0.30; llm\_control \ensuremath{\times} Gemini 0.29) --- a
\textbf{17--18 pp} gap that \emph{widened} under the larger harmonized sample (the earlier
N\ensuremath{\approx}100--500 optimistic estimates of 0.31--0.36 regressed to 0.29--0.30 at N=2000).
On RareBench HF the pattern holds: classical/offline (lirical 0.23, vc\_rdagent
0.28) and the HPO-pipeline deeprare (0.29--0.30) lead, while all other LLM
scaffolds sit \ensuremath{\leq}0.10. The RareBench gap is partly ORPHA-sibling mismatch in the
cross-map (Appendix A1 / F5).

\textbf{F2: Multi-agent scaffolding gives at most a small, dataset-dependent gain and often none, not a uniform boost.} On Gemini Flash (common N=2000), medagents
(PP-Store 0.30, RareArena 0.30) edges llm\_control (0.29, 0.28) by only \textasciitilde1--2 pp,
within the control's CI; mdagents (PP-Store 0.28) actually sits \textasciitilde1 pp \emph{below} the
control, and agentclinic/maidxo regress sharply (\Cref{sec-7-2}). The benefit does not
consistently exceed the no-scaffold control's CI and does not hold on every
backbone. (Revised down from the v0 ``+5--7 pp'' claim, which rested on a stale small-sample medagents 0.40.)

\textbf{F3: DeepSeek V4-Flash is \textasciitilde10\ensuremath{\times} cheaper than Gemini Flash but trades off accuracy.} Per-prediction cost \$0.00041 (V4-Flash) vs
\$0.00321 (Gemini), but V4-Flash R@1 is consistently lower: PP-Store \ensuremath{-}2 to \ensuremath{-}5 pp
(e.g.~medagents 0.25 vs 0.30; mdagents 0.25 vs 0.28). V4-Flash also showed a higher
transient empty-content rate on free-text/HPO-list inputs (mitigated by a
wrapper-level retry; see Appendix B). Conclusion: V4-Flash is the cost-efficient choice when \textasciitilde10\ensuremath{\times} cost reduction outweighs a \textasciitilde2--6 pp accuracy drop, but it does NOT match Gemini quality. (Reversed from v0.)

\textbf{F4: GPT-5 minimal-reasoning is not worth its cost, and at full-N has no scaffold where it is the sole winner.} GPT-5 (\texttt{reasoning\_effort=minimal},
forced because default reasoning consumes all max\_tokens) ties the field on
medagents PP-Store (0.28, where the medagents winner is Gemini at 0.30, V4-Pro-off at 0.28, GPT-5 at 0.28) and
yet collapses on AgentClinic OSCE dialogue (0.13, \ensuremath{-}10 pp vs Gemini). As the most expensive
backbone (\textasciitilde20\ensuremath{\times} V4-Flash per prediction) with no consistent accuracy edge, it is
hard to justify. The reasoning-channel question is answered directly by our H6
ablation (\Cref{sec-8}): on the single-call LLM control, turning reasoning on (V4-Pro)
changes R@1 by +0.008 (noise) while producing no parseable answer in 40\% of
cases and running 10--40\ensuremath{\times} slower --- thinking mode is not worth it on this task.

\textbf{F5: RareBench HF is uniquely hard for general LLM scaffolds (\ensuremath{\leq}0.10 R@1) but tractable for classical/HPO-pipeline agents (lirical 0.23, vc\_rdagent 0.28, deeprare 0.29--0.30).} Root causes: (a) RareBench gold labels use ORPHA codes
with sibling-disambiguation challenges across Orphanet's hierarchy
(\texttt{Methylmalonic\ acidemia\ with\ homocystinuria} ORPHA:26 vs \texttt{Vitamin\ B12-\ unresponsive\ methylmalonic\ acidemia} ORPHA:27 share concept but not OMIM
cross-ref); (b) classical/HPO agents use OMIM directly + Orphanet name fuzzy
match, bypassing this mismatch. A real evaluator-vs-data interaction, not a pure model failure. Adapter-side fuzzy variants logging recovers +1--8 pp but
doesn't close the gap.
